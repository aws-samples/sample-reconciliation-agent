Attached is the trade-level detail behind the four small differences you queried on the June revaluation. Short answer: these are FX rate rounding differences, not booking errors. The largest is USD 72.00 on a USD 2.6m notional, which is 0.0028%.

The cause is the number of decimal places each side carries on the rate. Coastline publish and store rates to five decimal places; your ledger appears to truncate to five as well but sources from a different snap time, so EUR/USD comes through as 1.08409 against our 1.08412. On a 2.4m EUR notional that three-pip difference is USD 72.00. The same mechanic explains GBP and CHF. JPY nets to zero because the rate agrees at the published precision.

Trade Ccy Custodian rate Ledger rate Difference (USD)
TR-771204 EUR 1.08412 1.08409 72.00
TR-771318 GBP 1.27055 1.27051 46.00
TR-771402 CHF 1.11840 1.11838 17.60
TR-771455 JPY 0.00662 0.00662 0.00
Total 135.60

All four sit well inside the USD 250 per-trade tolerance in the reconciliation policy, so they can be matched with the tolerance recorded. Please cite the custodian rate as the rate source when you record the tolerance applied — that is the rate that governs the actual cash movement.

One caveat worth carrying forward: a rounding difference should scale with notional. If you ever see a difference that is large relative to the notional, or one that stays constant across trades of very different sizes, it is not rounding — that pattern usually means the wrong rate date was applied, and it should be escalated rather than tolerated.
